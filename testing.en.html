00 · Daily plan frame

Composite for the map, pRTH and OVN for direction

The market moves from one balance area to another. The 30-day composite shows the balance areas (HVN) and the paths between them (LVN). Yesterday’s RTH (pRTH) and the overnight session (OVN) show where both sides are accepting price on that map, which gives the bias for the NY AM session.

TESTING PLAN

A hypothesis frame in forward test, not yet part of the main system. Use it alongside the daily TPO plan, with small size. The session log and the archived backtest of the old frame (RTH × OVN overlap) are left off the site for now.

Price inside an HVN

Fade range. Both sides agreed on price here. Sell the upper edge, buy the lower edge on a trigger, target the middle (the HVN POC).

Price accepted beyond an HVN edge

Continuation. Balance is broken. Price crosses the LVN fairly fast to the edge of the next HVN. Enter on the retest of the edge just broken, on M1.

Core rules

  • TPO 30' only flips the bias. One period closing beyond an HVN edge = the bias switches from fade to continuation. Two periods = more certain.
  • Enter and exit on M1 + footprint. Stop by M1 structure beyond the level. Do not wait for a period close to exit.
  • No entries in the middle of an LVN. An LVN is a path, not a place to wait.
  • Target = next HVN edge in the trade direction. If the distance there is under 1.5R, skip the trade.
  • The final direction is set at 10:00 ET by the open type and EMA 9/34 (5'). The bias from pRTH + OVN is only a pre-session hypothesis.
  • VWAP RTH is the in-session filter. Flat VWAP allows fading the HVN edges. Sloping VWAP with price riding a band allows only continuation or pullbacks, no fading (chapter 03).
  • Max 2–3 trades, stop at 11:30 ET. This frame has not been backtested: forward test with small size.

01 · Finding balance areas

An HVN is where price stays across many sessions. An LVN is where price only passes through.

There are two methods. Method 1 reads the composite directly in ATAS. Method 2 stacks the VAs of each RTH and OVN session: where many VAs overlap is an HVN. Method 2 is objective, does not depend on your eye, and matches your RTH + OVN reference idea. The best HVNs are where both methods agree.

Method 1 · Read the composite (5 steps)

  1. Smooth the profile: 30D composite with a larger row than the daily chart (about 10–20 points for NQ) to see the shape instead of a sawtooth.
  2. Find the “bulges”: a stretch where the profile keeps bulging, almost as thick as the thickest part nearby. Each bulge is an HVN.
  3. Find the “waist”: where the profile narrows between two bulges, down to about 1/3 of the bulge thickness or less. That is an LVN.
  4. Draw the HVN edges where the profile clearly starts to narrow, not at a lone spike. Draw a zone (box), not a line.
  5. Merge or drop: merge two bulges separated by a waist under about 40 points. A bulge narrower than about 100 points is a level, not a balance area.

Method 2 · Stack RTH + OVN VAs

  1. Take the VA (VAL–VAH) of every RTH and OVN session over the last 10–20 days.
  2. At each price, count how many VAs contain it.
  3. HVN = a price stretch with 3 VA or more stacked.
  4. LVN = the gap between two HVNs. An LVN is clear when no VA covers it.
  5. Mode RTH ∩ OVN: keeps only areas where both RTH VAs and OVN VAs stack heavily. That is balance accepted by both groups.

ZoneFromToWideNote

Check whether an HVN is “real”

  • At least 2–3 session TPOCs (RTH or OVN) sit inside the zone.
  • Price has returned to this zone many times, not just passed through once.
  • Both the composite (method 1) and VA stacking (method 2) show this zone.
  • At least about half the average RTH range wide. The RTH range is currently about 250–350 points, so an HVN narrower than about 120 points should be treated as a level.

Common mistakes

  • Treating every spike in the profile as an HVN. Then price is always "near an HVN" and zones lose meaning.
  • Using too long a composite when the market runs hard. The last 30 days span about 5000 points and old zones may no longer matter. Prefer 10–20 days then.
  • Drawing the HVN edge as a thin line and entering exactly at it. An edge is a zone; enter on a trigger, not on the touch.
  • Forgetting to update: when price builds new value inside an LVN for 2–3 sessions, that LVN is becoming an HVN.

03 · VWAP RTH

VWAP RTH: the in-session compass, laid over the HVN map

Composite, pRTH and OVN answer the pre-session question: where price is on the map and which way to lean. VWAP RTH answers the in-session question: since 9:30 today, which side is in control. Location picks the trade type (Fade or Continuation); VWAP decides whether that trade is allowed.

Definition and setup

  • VWAP = Σ(price × volume) / Σ volume, from 9:30 ET (21:30 VN). Same anchor as RTH and the IB, so all three tools speak the same time language.
  • Band ±1σ / ±2σ / ±3σ = volume-weighted standard deviation around VWAP. ±1σ is normal rotation, ±2σ and ±3σ are stretched.
  • Put it on the M1 execution. VWAP ETH (anchor 18:00) is only a reference when overnight volume is unusual, never the filter.
  • Price above VWAP: today’s average buyer is in profit, buyers control the session. Below VWAP, the reverse.
  • In the first 15–20 minutes σ is small and jumpy, so the bands are very tight. Do not fade the bands before 10:00 ET.

VWAP × the HVN map

Flat VWAP inside the HVN

Balance on both the larger frame and in session. Fade both edges, target VWAP or the HVN POC.

±2 SD band on an HVN edge (within 10–15 pts)

A stronger edge. If VWAP is flat, this is the top fade spot of the day.

HVN edge breaks, VWAP slopes with the break

A trustworthy continuation. A pullback to VWAP / ±1σ or an edge retest is the entry, target the next HVN edge.

Beyond the edge but VWAP flat

Weak breakout. Price often returns to VWAP and a period closes back inside the HVN. Do not chase; see whether a fade sets up.

Whatever the setup, the distance to the first target must still be ≥ 1.5R.

Common mistakes

  • Fading ±2σ on an Open Drive day while VWAP slopes. It is a trend day; the band is where to add, not where it reverses.
  • Treating a VWAP touch as a signal. A touch is only location; you still need a delta / footprint trigger on M1.
  • Confusing VWAP ETH with VWAP RTH on the M1 chart.
  • Fading the bands before 10:00 ET while σ is still too tight.

05 · Trading process

Three setups, one direction, entries on M1

The pre-session bias is a hypothesis. At 10:00 ET the open type and EMA 9/34 set the direction. After that only three setups are allowed.

Set the direction at 10:00 ET (22:00 VN)

Directional OD / OTD / ORRSame directionPrice holds the open-type side, VWAP starts sloping with itTrade in the open-type direction. If it is against the pre-session bias, the open type wins.
Directional OD / OTD / ORROpposite directionAnyDo not trade a direction. Only fade inside the HVN on a trigger.

F Fade inside the HVN

When
Price inside the HVN, OA open type or a bias pointing into the zone, flat VWAP RTH. An HVN edge on the ±2σ band is the best fade.
Entry
Price reaches an HVN edge (or a pRTH VA edge inside the HVN). Within 5 M1 candles one closes back inside with delta against the touch, or absorption.
Stop
Beyond the new extreme, on M1.
Target
The HVN POC, then the other edge.
Skip when
OD open type toward the edge, VWAP sloping outward, or price touching the edge for the 3rd time.

C Continuation through the LVN

When
One 30' period closes beyond the HVN edge in the bias direction (the bias switches to continuation), with VWAP RTH sloping in the break direction.
Entry
Retest of the edge just broken on M1: the candle closes holding outside, delta aligned, EMA 9 > 34 (long).
Stop
Back inside the HVN on M1.
Target
The near edge of the next HVN. Skip if under 1.5R.
Skip when
Next period closes back inside the HVN, or price crosses back through VWAP: failed breakout, back to fading.

V Pullback to VWAP

When
After 10:00, price has held one side of VWAP for at least 15 minutes, in the bias direction.
Entry
First VWAP touch, with a delta trigger and EMA 9/34 aligned.
Target
Session high/low, or the next HVN edge.
Skip when
Third VWAP touch, or 2 M1 candles closing through VWAP against the trade.
Note
Best setup in the old-frame backtest (45%, sample too small).

Trade and risk management

  • The stop lives on M1. Do not move it to wait for a 30' close.
  • Exit at target, stop or 11:30 ET, whichever comes first. Do not hold past 11:30 without a rule written before the session.
  • After a stop-out at an edge, do not re-enter that edge unless the bias has flipped and price retests from the other side.
  • Max 3 trades. Stop after 2 losses in a row. No entries in the middle of an LVN.
06 · Example

Session 16/11: pRTH and OVN both accepted lower, price at the lower HVN edge

Numbers read approximately from two charts (your composite and your friend’s chart), only to illustrate the reading. Check them on ATAS before using them as data.

Pre-session read

  1. Composite: three HVNs around 26840–27370, 27620–28040, 28200–28440. The clearest LVN is 27370–27620.
  2. pRTH (13/11): fell hard from the upper HVN, built value in the middle HVN, closed near the low. Accepted lower.
  3. OVN (16/11): gapped up to about 28260 but was sold back, VA about 27660–27880, below the pRTH VA. Skewed down.
  4. Price before the open about 27600: near the lower edge of the middle HVN.
  5. Bias: pRTH down + OVN down = A reinforced. Near the lower edge with the bias pointing out → prefer Continuation down, target the upper edge of the lower HVN at 27370.

How it played out and the entry

  1. RTH opened below the 27620 edge and moved down inside the LVN.
  2. A period close below 27620 confirms the continuation bias. The entry is on the bounce retesting 27620 on M1, with negative delta.
  3. Price ran through the LVN to about 27350, right at the upper edge of the lower HVN (27370). Target hit.
  4. At 27370 switch to fade thinking: price is back at the lower HVN and the edge is where to wait for a reversal on a trigger. The arrow on your friend’s chart (down to 27370, then bouncing) is exactly this scenario.
