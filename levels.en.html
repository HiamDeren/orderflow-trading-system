01 · Foundations

What TPO is and how to read a profile

TPO (Time Price Opportunity) records every price at which the market was "present" in each 30-minute period. Stack those periods horizontally and you get a distribution: thick areas are where the market accepts price (value), thin areas are where it rejects or passes through very quickly. A candle tells you how price moved, the profile tells you where price was accepted .

Reading the sample profile on the left

  1. Open in the A period, A pushes higher then gets sold hard: 4 rows with only the letter A at the top = selling tail. Responsive sellers rejected the higher prices. This high is "complete".
  2. IB = A + B. C breaks the IB low = downside range extension. Initiative sellers appear.
  3. C moves fast and leaves single prints in the middle of the profile: an imbalance area, often resistance when price comes back, or it gets filled if the market changes its mind.
  4. The lower part builds thick value (D → M): POC sits low, the profile has the shape of the letter b → long liquidation.
  5. The low has 3 different letters touching the same price, no tail: poor low. The auction below is not finished, the next day can easily come back to sweep it.

Rotation Factor: compute it yourself in 2 minutes

Compare each period with the one just before it. Higher high: +1, lower: −1, equal: 0. Do the same for the Low. Add everything up.

PeriodHighLowHigh scoreLow scoreCumulative

Large positive RF = upward rotation dominates (buyers in control). Near 0 = two-sided, balance. Use the RF of A–C to confirm the open type, not as an entry signal.

Answers to the "open questions" in your Lecture 5 recap

  • Tail: 1 print or 2 prints? The two counts run in different directions. "Two prints at the top" in the video means two different letters touching the highest price (counted horizontally) → that is a poor high, so not a tail. A tail is counted vertically: the number of consecutive rows at the extreme with a single letter. According to Dalton, the minimum excess is 2 single-print rows. For NQ, a practical convention: tail ≥ 2 rows at the row size you use (e.g. 5 points/row → tail ≥ 10 points).
  • 68.4% or 70%? 1 standard deviation of a normal distribution ≈ 68.27%, that is the mathematical root. 70% is the CBOT rounding convention and the default on most platforms, including ATAS. Pick one number and keep it fixed so days are comparable.
  • How is RF calculated? The table just above.
  • "Key contextual level" what is it? In this playbook it means the Tier 1–2 levels in tab 04 (weekly VA, prior day VAH/VAL/POC, naked POC, poor high/low, single prints). Price closing through such a level during the IB usually makes the IB wide and one-directional.

03 · Initial Balance

The first 60 minutes decide the structure of the whole day

Initial Balance (IB) is the range of periods A + B, i.e. 9:30–10:30 ET = 21:30–22:30 Vietnam time (winter). In your system the IB has replaced Range69 as the structural anchor, and shares its anchor with VWAP RTH and the CVD reset. The first half of your 2-hour window is when the IB is forming. The second half (C–D, 10:30–11:30 ET) is when the first range extension happens or fails.

Components

ComponentCalculationRole
IBH / IBLHigh / Low of 9:30–10:30 ETRange extension boundary, Tier 3 level

IB mid(IBH + IBL) / 2Who is winning the IB, target of rotations

There are two conventions for extensions. The playbook uses the "total range" convention (1.5x up = IBL + 1.5 × width = IBH + 0.5 × width). Some sources use IBH + N × width. Pick one convention, label it on the chart and in the journal, never mix them.

Why the IB matters

1 The day’s first balance

In the first hour most trading comes from day-timeframe traders reacting to the open and overnight. They create the first area of balance. It is a "mini" version of the value area.

2 IB break = someone bigger is here

When price leaves the IB after 10:30 it is usually the other timeframe (funds, multi-day position holders) acting. One-sided range extension is initiative activity on that side.

3 Width tells you about the rest of the day

Wide IB: the large timeframe came in early, most of the day’s range is already in. Narrow IB: nobody has committed yet, energy is building for range extension. You have this information right at 10:30.

Common IB mistakes

  • Entering the breakout as soon as price touches IBH at 10:31, without waiting for acceptance or a retest. Most first touches are probes.
  • Fading the breakout of a narrow IB. That goes against the structure exactly when it has the highest trend probability.
  • Forgetting the 10:00 ET news (ISM, JOLTS, Consumer Confidence…). News distorts period B so the IB width is no longer representative. Note it in the journal.
  • A wrong ATAS timezone shifting the IB by 1 hour (especially in DST change weeks). Check that the IB bar starts exactly at 9:30 ET.
  • Mixing the two extension conventions between the chart and the journal.
  • Treating the IB mid as strong as IBH/IBL. The IB mid is a target and a place to move the stop, not a main entry zone.
  • Trading the Neutral scenario (both sides broken) in your window. Neutral usually only completes after 11:30, so use it as context for the next day.

05 · Add-in confluence #1

VWAP RTH: the dynamic value of today’s session

TPO tells you where value has been built (usually yesterday’s). VWAP RTH tells you the average cost of everyone who has traded since 9:30 today. Combine the two: TPO is the map, VWAP is the in-session compass.

Definition

VWAP(t) = Σ (Pᵢ × Vᵢ) / Σ Vᵢ with i running from 9:30 ET to t σ(t) = √[ Σ Vᵢ × (Pᵢ − VWAP)² / Σ Vᵢ ] Band = VWAP ± k·σ, k = 1, 2, 3
  • Anchor 9:30 ET: the same anchor as the IB and TPO RTH, so the three tools speak the same time language. VWAP ETH (18:00 ET) is only a secondary layer when overnight volume is unusual, as you decided.
  • Price above VWAP: today’s average buyer is in profit → buyers control intraday. Below VWAP the opposite.
  • First 15–20 minutes σ is still small and jumps constantly, the bands are very narrow. Do not fade the bands before 10:00 ET.

The four VWAP states

StateRecognitionAction
BalanceFlat VWAP, price crosses back and forth many times, stays within ±1 SDPing-pong ±1 SD, fade ±2/±3 SD back to VWAP
UptrendVWAP slopes up, price holds above VWAP, rides along +1/+2 SDBuy pullbacks to VWAP / +1 SD. Never short +2 SD
DowntrendMirror imageSell pullbacks to VWAP / −1 SD
Phase changePrice reclaims / loses VWAP after the IB, VWAP starts changing slopeTrade the VWAP retest in the new direction, needs CVD confirmation

VWAP × TPO: read them together

VWAP ≈ dPOC

Time and volume agree on the same price → balance. Fade the developing VA edges and ±2 SD.

VWAP leads, dPOC follows

dPOC moves in the same direction as VWAP after 10:30 → value is migrating, the day is likely a trend or normal variation day. Go with it.

VWAP matches a TPO level

VWAP or the ±2 SD band within 10–15 points of yesterday’s VAH/VAL/POC → zone A per the scoring table in tab 04.

Common mistakes

  • Fading ±2 SD on an Open Drive + IB extension day. That is a trend day, the bands are only places to add.
  • Confusing VWAP ETH with VWAP RTH on the M1 chart. Give them clearly different names and colours.
  • Using VWAP as an independent level, ignoring TPO. VWAP only adds points when it coincides with a location.
  • Treating a VWAP touch as a signal. A touch is only a location, you still need CVD + a trigger.
06 · Add-in confluence #2

CVD divergence: who is aggressive, and can they still move price?

You said your main weakness right now is reading footprint/delta inaccurately. CVD is the simplification: instead of reading every bid × ask cell, just compare two consecutive swings of price with the two matching CVD swings, and only at a TPO level.

Definition

Delta(bar) = Volume filled at the Ask (market buy) − Volume filled at the Bid (market sell) CVD(t) = Σ Delta from the reset point to t Reset = 9:30 ET (same anchor as VWAP RTH and TPO RTH)
  • Aggressive (initiator): market orders, they create delta. Passive: limit orders on the DOM, they absorb delta.
  • Price moves when aggression exceeds the passive liquidity. Divergence happens when the two are out of line.
  • Compare CVD by the swing shape within the same session, not absolute values across days.

Mandatory filters

  1. Location first: only consider divergence at a zone scoring ≥ 4 (tab 04). Divergence in mid-air = noise.
  2. Regime: on trend days (Open Drive, IB extension, sloping VWAP) counter-trend divergence fails repeatedly. Only accept it when it coincides with Tier 1 + the ±2 SD band.
  3. Trigger: divergence is not an entry. Wait for an M1 micro-structure break, a level reclaim, or a delta flip.
  4. Invalidation: beyond the extreme that created the divergence. Price going past it means the idea is wrong.

The 4-type divergence matrix

Top panel: price. Bottom panel: CVD. Purple line = TPO level. The two "exhaustion" types are the aggressor weakening; the two "absorption" types are the aggressor still strong but blocked by passive orders. Absorption is usually stronger because a large player is standing at the level.
