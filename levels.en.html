01 · Foundations

What TPO is and how to read a profile

TPO (Time Price Opportunity) records every price at which the market was "present" in each 30-minute period. Stack those periods horizontally and you get a distribution: thick areas are where the market accepts price (value), thin areas are where it rejects or passes through very quickly. A candle tells you how price moved, the profile tells you where price was accepted .

Reading the sample profile on the left

  1. Open in the A period, A pushes higher then gets sold hard: 4 rows with only the letter A at the top = selling tail. Responsive sellers rejected the higher prices. This high is "complete".
  2. IB = A + B. C breaks the IB low = downside range extension. Initiative sellers appear.
  3. C moves fast and leaves single prints in the middle of the profile: an imbalance area, often resistance when price comes back, or it gets filled if the market changes its mind.
  4. The lower part builds thick value (D → M): POC sits low, the profile has the shape of the letter b → long liquidation.
  5. The low has 3 different letters touching the same price, no tail: poor low. The auction below is not finished, the next day can easily come back to sweep it.

Rotation Factor: compute it yourself in 2 minutes

Compare each period with the one just before it. Higher high: +1, lower: −1, equal: 0. Do the same for the Low. Add everything up.

PeriodHighLowHigh scoreLow scoreCumulative

Large positive RF = upward rotation dominates (buyers in control). Near 0 = two-sided, balance. Use the RF of A–C to confirm the open type, not as an entry signal.

Answers to the "open questions" in your Lecture 5 recap

  • Tail: 1 print or 2 prints? The two counts run in different directions. "Two prints at the top" in the video means two different letters touching the highest price (counted horizontally) → that is a poor high, so not a tail. A tail is counted vertically: the number of consecutive rows at the extreme with a single letter. According to Dalton, the minimum excess is 2 single-print rows. For NQ, a practical convention: tail ≥ 2 rows at the row size you use (e.g. 5 points/row → tail ≥ 10 points).
  • 68.4% or 70%? 1 standard deviation of a normal distribution ≈ 68.27%, that is the mathematical root. 70% is the CBOT rounding convention and the default on most platforms, including ATAS. Pick one number and keep it fixed so days are comparable.
  • How is RF calculated? The table just above.
  • "Key contextual level" what is it? In this playbook it means the Tier 1–2 levels in tab 04 (weekly VA, prior day VAH/VAL/POC, naked POC, poor high/low, single prints). Price closing through such a level during the IB usually makes the IB wide and one-directional.

03 · Initial Balance

The first 60 minutes decide the structure of the whole day

Initial Balance (IB) is the range of periods A + B, i.e. 9:30–10:30 ET = 21:30–22:30 Vietnam time (winter). In your system the IB has replaced Range69 as the structural anchor, and shares its anchor with VWAP RTH and the CVD reset. The first half of your 2-hour window is when the IB is forming. The second half (C–D, 10:30–11:30 ET) is when the first range extension happens or fails.

There are two conventions for extensions. The playbook uses the "total range" convention (1.5x up = IBL + 1.5 × width = IBH + 0.5 × width). Some sources use IBH + N × width. Pick one convention, label it on the chart and in the journal, never mix them.

Why the IB matters

1 The day’s first balance

In the first hour most trading comes from day-timeframe traders reacting to the open and overnight. They create the first area of balance. It is a "mini" version of the value area.

2 IB break = someone bigger is here

When price leaves the IB after 10:30 it is usually the other timeframe (funds, multi-day position holders) acting. One-sided range extension is initiative activity on that side.

3 Width tells you about the rest of the day

Wide IB: the large timeframe came in early, most of the day’s range is already in. Narrow IB: nobody has committed yet, energy is building for range extension. You have this information right at 10:30.

Common IB mistakes

  • Entering the breakout as soon as price touches IBH at 10:31, without waiting for acceptance or a retest. Most first touches are probes.
  • Fading the breakout of a narrow IB. That goes against the structure exactly when it has the highest trend probability.
  • Forgetting the 10:00 ET news (ISM, JOLTS, Consumer Confidence…). News distorts period B so the IB width is no longer representative. Note it in the journal.
  • A wrong ATAS timezone shifting the IB by 1 hour (especially in DST change weeks). Check that the IB bar starts exactly at 9:30 ET.
  • Mixing the two extension conventions between the chart and the journal.
  • Treating the IB mid as strong as IBH/IBL. The IB mid is a target and a place to move the stop, not a main entry zone.
  • Trading the Neutral scenario (both sides broken) in your window. Neutral usually only completes after 11:30, so use it as context for the next day.

05 · Add-in confluence #1

VWAP RTH: the dynamic value of today’s session

TPO tells you where value has been built (usually yesterday’s). VWAP RTH tells you the average cost of everyone who has traded since 9:30 today. Combine the two: TPO is the map, VWAP is the in-session compass.

Definition

VWAP(t) = Σ (Pᵢ × Vᵢ) / Σ Vᵢ with i running from 9:30 ET to t σ(t) = √[ Σ Vᵢ × (Pᵢ − VWAP)² / Σ Vᵢ ] Band = VWAP ± k·σ, k = 1, 2, 3
  • Anchor 9:30 ET: the same anchor as the IB and TPO RTH, so the three tools speak the same time language. VWAP ETH (18:00 ET) is only a secondary layer when overnight volume is unusual, as you decided.
  • Price above VWAP: today’s average buyer is in profit → buyers control intraday. Below VWAP the opposite.
  • First 15–20 minutes σ is still small and jumps constantly, the bands are very narrow. Do not fade the bands before 10:00 ET.

The four VWAP states

StateRecognitionAction
BalanceFlat VWAP, price crosses back and forth many times, stays within ±1 SDPing-pong ±1 SD, fade ±2/±3 SD back to VWAP
UptrendVWAP slopes up, price holds above VWAP, rides along +1/+2 SDBuy pullbacks to VWAP / +1 SD. Never short +2 SD
DowntrendMirror imageSell pullbacks to VWAP / −1 SD
Phase changePrice reclaims / loses VWAP after the IB, VWAP starts changing slopeTrade the VWAP retest in the new direction, needs CVD confirmation

VWAP × TPO: read them together

VWAP ≈ dPOC

Time and volume agree on the same price → balance. Fade the developing VA edges and ±2 SD.

VWAP leads, dPOC follows

dPOC moves in the same direction as VWAP after 10:30 → value is migrating, the day is likely a trend or normal variation day. Go with it.

VWAP matches a TPO level

VWAP or the ±2 SD band within 10–15 points of yesterday’s VAH/VAL/POC → zone A per the scoring table in tab 04.

Common mistakes

  • Fading ±2 SD on an Open Drive + IB extension day. That is a trend day, the bands are only places to add.
  • Confusing VWAP ETH with VWAP RTH on the M1 chart. Give them clearly different names and colours.
  • Using VWAP as an independent level, ignoring TPO. VWAP only adds points when it coincides with a location.
  • Treating a VWAP touch as a signal. A touch is only a location, you still need CVD + a trigger.

EMA 9/34: market state, directional permission and the hold rule

The EMA is not an entry signal. In your system it does three things: read the state of the market, grant permission to trade a direction, and decide whether to hold or cut the trade. 6.1

Definition & formula

Understand the mechanics to know how much the EMA lags and how fast it reacts. Only then do you know when to trust it.

EMA (Exponential Moving Average) is a weighted moving average: the newer the candle, the larger its weight; weights decay exponentially into the past and never quite reach 0.

Formula

EMAt = α · Closet + (1 − α) · EMAt−1
α = 2 / (N + 1)

Initialisation: the first EMA = SMA of the first N candles. On each new candle the EMA moves toward the close by α × the distance.

EMA9 example (α = 0.2): old EMA 27,250, candle closes 27,270 → new EMA = 27,250 + 0.2 × 20 = 27.254.

Cyan bars are the latest N candles, grey bars are the older tail. Try N = 9, then N = 34: the N candles always carry about 86.5%. That is why “EMA9 ≈ 9-candle average” sounds right but is not exact.

6.2

8 core concepts

Every EMA decision comes down to these 8 questions. Read the chart in order from 1 to 8.

01 · Close location
Where does the candle close?

Only counts body close versus EMA9. A wick that pierces and closes back is a reaction, not yet a change.

02 · Slope
Slope

A clearly sloping EMA9 means momentum. A flat EMA9 means the market is balancing. EMA34 flipping slope means the underlying trend is changing.

03 · Stack
Order

Bull: Price > 9 > 34. Bear: Price < 9 < 34. Mixed means transition.

04 · Spread
9–34 spread

Widening means the trend is accelerating. Narrowing means momentum is fading. Braided lines mean a range.

05 · Cross
Cross

Always comes sau the top/bottom. Use it to confirm the state has changed, not as an entry point.

06 · Dynamic S/R
Dynamic support / resistance

Strong trend: price retests EMA9. Normal trend: price returns to the zone between 9 and 34. A close through 34 means the trend is in trouble.

07 · Extension
Overextended

When price is unusually far from EMA9, do not chase. Wait for price to come back to the EMA or a PD array.

08 · Compression
Compressed

9 and 34 merge and go flat: no signal left. Switch to trading TPO edges with orderflow.

Anatomy of an uptrend leg
EMA9EMA34cross

① Bullish cross confirms the trend sau after price has already run. ② Spread widens, price hugs EMA9: strong trend. ③ Price pulls back into 9–34 and holds: healthy pullback. ④ Spread narrows, EMA9 flattens: momentum is fading.

6.3

5 market states (S1–S5)

Before every decision, label the state. Each state has its own default action. If you cannot label it, treat it as S3 or S4 and stand aside.

Sign

  • Correct stack: price > 9 > 34 (or the reverse in a downtrend)
  • Spread widening, both lines sloping
  • Almost no candle closes on the wrong side of EMA9

Action

  • Trade with the trend only. Do not fade.
  • Entry: retest of EMA9 or an FVG near EMA9 (model E1)
  • Do not chase when price is far from EMA9

Sign

  • Candles close through EMA9 but price holds the zone between 9 and 34
  • EMA34 still sloping, no cross
  • Structured pullback (small lower high in an uptrend)

Action

  • Wait for price to enter the 9–34 zone aligns TPO zone or FVG
  • Trigger with OF: absorption, delta flip (model E2)
  • Stop beyond EMA34 and beyond the zone

Sign

  • Price stuck between 9 and 34, closing back and forth across EMA9
  • EMA9 flips slope, spread narrows fast
  • Cross about to happen or just happened

Action

  • Reduce size or stand aside
  • The nearest PD array (SIBI/BISI) decides the direction
  • This is when you need more information before acting

Sign

  • Candles keep closing back and forth across EMA9

6.4

Confirm entry: 4 models + 5-layer checklist

The EMA never pulls the trigger. Every model below needs a location (zone/PD array) and an orderflow trigger. The EMA only answers “am I allowed” and “how long do I hold”.

E1 · Retest EMA9 Continuation State S1

Idea: in a strong trend EMA9 is a dynamic “floor”. Price touching EMA9 for the first time, right where there is an FVG or imbalance, is the lowest-risk entry.

TriggerWick touches/pierces EMA9, candle closes back on the right side + OF confirms (absorption at the wick low, delta flip)
StopBeyond the nearest swing. If EMA34 is closer, place it beyond EMA34
TargetPrevious swing high/low, next DOL; runner follows the EMA9 ladder
InvalidationA close through EMA34, or the spread narrowing fast (S1 turning into S3)

Trap: the 3rd–4th retest in the same leg has much lower odds than the first. Only take the first retest after each new high/low.

E2 · Pullback into 9–34 at a key level Continuation State S2

Idea: a deeper pullback than E1. Price enters the “cloud” between the two EMAs. Only take it when the cloud aligns TPO zone (VAH/VAL/POC) or an M5–M15 FVG.

TriggerPrice enters the cloud + touches the zone, a rejection candle closes back across EMA9 + delta flip / stacked imbalance in the trade direction
StopBeyond EMA34 and beyond the zone, whichever is farther
TargetPrior high/low of the trend; TP1 at 1R or at a structural level
InvalidationA decisive close beyond EMA34 and beyond the zone; EMA34 flat or flipping

Fit with the system: this is exactly the “VAH/VAL holds” read from the TPO chapters, with EMA34 added to confirm the underlying trend is still alive.

E3 · Sweep → Reclaim EMA9 → PD array Reversal State S5

Idea: the reversal-after-sweep model. After liquidity is swept, a candle closing back across EMA9 signals a momentum shift. A SIBI/BISI that holds is where the order goes.

Trigger① Sweep BSL/SSL → ② a displacement candle closes back across EMA9 → ③ price retests the SIBI/BISI/IFVG and closes back below EMA9 (short) + OF at the PD array
StopAbove the sweep high (safe) or above the SIBI (tight)
TargetNext key level: TPO VAL/POC, round number, low of the up leg; runner to the DOL
InvalidationCandle closes above the SIBI. At that point the PD array is broken and the bias flips
When the EMA and the SIBI disagree (candle closes above 9 and 34 but still below the SIBI): do not add, do not panic-exit. Cut to half, stop above the SIBI, read the footprint at the SIBI. Seller absorption and negative delta at the high mean the SIBI wins. Buy stacked imbalance through the SIBI means the EMA wins.
E4 · First pullback sau cross Filter S5 turning into S1/S2

Idea: the cross is not a trigger, but a clean (after a sweep, with displacement) opens one opportunity: the first pullback into the 9–34 cloud after the cross.

TriggerFirst pullback after the cross touches the 9–34 cloud at a PD array + OF confirms
StopBeyond the swing that created the cross
TargetExtreme of the displacement leg, then the DOL
Invalidation3 or more crosses in 30 candles (S4); or price closes back through EMA34 before target 1

S4 filter: before considering E4, count crosses in the last 30 candles. 3 or more means skip it and go back to trading range edges.

6.5

Managing trades with EMA9: a 4-step ladder

The rule “close below EMA9 means hold, close above means cut”, written as 4 concrete steps. The example below is a short; reverse it for a long.

  1. Hold: candle closes below EMA9. The rule starts from the candle sau the trigger candle. At +1R, move the stop above the most recent candle high.
  2. Warning: candle first closes above EMA9. Take 50% or move the stop to breakeven. Choose one when writing the plan, not while in the trade.
  3. Exit all: a 2nd consecutive candle closes above EMA9, or 1 candle closes above EMA34.
  4. PD array exception: a close above EMA9 but still below the SIBI keeps the remainder, stop above the SIBI, read OF. A close above the SIBI exits everything, no exceptions.

Why this ladder suits prop accounts

The biggest risk to a prop account is the trailing drawdown. It trails unrealised profit, so letting a winner fall back to breakeven or a loss is what loses the account. Step 2 locks in part of the profit as soon as momentum shows signs of turning.

Trade-off: you will sometimes be shaken out of a long trend. Backtest to learn whether it helps or hurts on NQ M1; do not guess.

Example: one short trade from start to finish

Chart markers are computed from the data: the first candle closing above EMA9 after entry, and the candle closing above EMA34.

Do not use the ladder in S4 (range). In a range candles keep closing back and forth across EMA9, so the ladder kicks you out of every trade. In a range, manage by structure: stop beyond the range edge, target the POC or the opposite edge.
6.6

Common mistakes

Most EMA mistakes are not about the formula. They are about using the EMA for the right question.

Do

  • Label the state S1–S5 first when thinking about entry
  • Only the close versus the EMA counts, ignore wicks
  • Treat the EMA as trade permission and the hold rule; the trigger is OF
  • Prioritise the PD array over the EMA when the two disagree
  • Pick the timeframe (30s/1m/2m) before the session and keep it
  • Lock the 9/34 period while backtesting, note it in the journal

Don’t

  • Entering just because 9 crossed 34
  • Chasing price when it is far from EMA9
  • Using the EMA as a signal when both lines are flat and braided (S4)
  • Switching to 30s mid-trade because 30s candles “close nicer”
  • Tweaking 9 → 8 → 10 after every loss. Still the strategy-hopping mistake, just at a smaller scale
  • Fading an S1 trend just because price “has gone up a lot”, with no sweep and no candle closing back across EMA9

07 · Add-in confluence #3

CVD divergence: who is aggressive, and can they still move price?

You said your main weakness right now is reading footprint/delta inaccurately. CVD is the simplification: instead of reading every bid × ask cell, just compare two consecutive swings of price with the two matching CVD swings, and only at a TPO level.

Definition

Delta(bar) = Volume filled at the Ask (market buy) − Volume filled at the Bid (market sell) CVD(t) = Σ Delta from the reset point to t Reset = 9:30 ET (same anchor as VWAP RTH and TPO RTH)
  • Aggressive (initiator): market orders, they create delta. Passive: limit orders on the DOM, they absorb delta.
  • Price moves when aggression exceeds the passive liquidity. Divergence happens when the two are out of line.
  • Compare CVD by the swing shape within the same session, not absolute values across days.

Mandatory filters

  1. Location first: only consider divergence at a zone scoring ≥ 4 (tab 04). Divergence in mid-air = noise.
  2. Regime: on trend days (Open Drive, IB extension, sloping VWAP) counter-trend divergence fails repeatedly. Only accept it when it coincides with Tier 1 + the ±2 SD band.
  3. Trigger: divergence is not an entry. Wait for an M1 micro-structure break, a level reclaim, or a delta flip.
  4. Invalidation: beyond the extreme that created the divergence. Price going past it means the idea is wrong.

The 4-type divergence matrix

Top panel: price. Bottom panel: CVD. Purple line = TPO level. The two "exhaustion" types are the aggressor weakening; the two "absorption" types are the aggressor still strong but blocked by passive orders. Absorption is usually stronger because a large player is standing at the level.
