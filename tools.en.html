01

Foundations: market orders, limit orders and the auction

Every orderflow tool measures one of two things: resting orders (passive) or orders filled by the aggressive side (aggressive).

Orderflow is reading the actual flow of orders being filled and resting to see which side controls price at a specific level, instead of only looking at the result, the candle.

Limit order = passive

Rests at a price, waiting for someone else to fill it. Buy limits sit on the bid, sell limits on the ask. Shown on the DOM. Can be pulled at any time, so it can be a spoof.

Market order = aggressor

Accepts the other side’s price to fill immediately. Market buys fill on the ask, market sells fill on the bid. Shown on the tape and footprint after the fill.

How price moves

  1. Price only ticks up when all sell limits at the current ask are eaten by market buys, or pulled by whoever placed them.
  2. So market orders are the side that creates movement, limit orders are the side that blocks it or lets it through.
  3. Heavy market buying without price rising means a large sell limit is absorbing: this is the root of the absorption concept.
  4. Price moves fast through areas with few resting orders (LVN) and slows down in areas with many orders and much volume (HVN). This is the link between orderflow and TPO/volume profile.

Two kinds of activity in Auction Market Theory

Initiative

The aggressive side pushes price out of the value area and it gets accepted. Orderflow: stacked imbalance, CVD confirms, fast tape. Fits the playbook Break.

Responsive

Price leaves value and gets pushed back. Orderflow: absorption, exhaustion, divergence, delta flip. Fits the playbook Fade.

02

DOM: the order book

ATAS: Smart DOM. Used in phase 3 when price touches the level.

DOM (Depth of Market) shows the number of limit contracts resting at each price around the current price: the bid side (resting buys) and the ask side (resting sells). The DOM shows passive liquidity, not trades that have happened.

Concepts

Stacking

Resting orders added at a price as price approaches. Someone wants to defend that level.

Pulling

Resting orders pulled just before price touches. An open road for price to pass.

Wall

A price with unusually large size compared with its neighbours. Only meaningful when volume trades into it and it still stands.

Spoof

A large order placed to create an impression, then pulled before it is filled. The reason not to trust the DOM alone.

Iceberg / refill

An order that shows only part of its size. Volume traded at the price far exceeds the displayed size, and the size keeps refilling.

Heatmap

DOM history over time (ATAS: DOM Levels). Shows how long a wall has existed and whether it was pulled before price arrived.

How to read

  1. Only watch the DOM when price is a few ticks from the chosen level. Do not use the DOM to find setups.
  2. Compare size at the level with when price was far away: an increase is stacking, a decrease without traded volume is pulling.
  3. Cross-check with the tape: size decreasing because of fills (the wall being eaten) is completely different from size decreasing because of pulling.
  4. Watch the opposite side too: a long fade needs the ask above to thin out; a long break needs the bid below to follow price up.

Examples

A. Stacking: the level holds

VAL 25,410.50. At T1 price is 6 ticks away, bid at the level is 140. At T2 price touches, the bid rises to 360 even though volume has traded into it; the ask above shrinks. Supports a fade long if the footprint gives a trigger.

B. Pulling: the level breaks

Same VAL. At T1 the bid at the level is 300. At T2, with price 1 tick away, the bid is only 18 with almost no traded volume: the orders were pulled. Do not fade; prepare for the scenario break down.

C. Iceberg on the bid

The DOM always shows 12 lots at 25,410.50 but the tape shows 186 lots traded at this price and price has not broken. A large player is absorbing with a hidden order. An iceberg at an extreme usually holds price; an iceberg that gets broken usually leads to a strong move in the break direction.

Do and don’t

Do
  • Use the DOM to fine-tune entry at a level chosen in advance.
  • Always confirm with the tape: are there real fills?
  • Note whether the opposite side is thin or thick.
Don’t
  • Enter just because you see a big wall.
  • Watching the DOM around the 8:30, 10:00 ET news or the 9:30 spike.
  • Watching the DOM continuously all session, which causes fatigue and overtrading.
03

Tape: time & sales

ATAS: Smart Tape (2 columns Price + Volume, coloured by aggressor side). Used in phases 2 and 3.

Tape (Time & Sales) lists every filled trade: price, size, aggressor side (buy at the ask or sell at the bid) and time. Smart Tape merges orders split into pieces at the same moment into one line so you see the real size.

Concepts

Print

One line on the tape. Green: aggressive buy filled at the ask. Red: aggressive sell filled at the bid.

Size filter

Show only orders above a threshold. Set it by session percentile (e.g. top 1%), tuned separately for RTH and ETH.

Big trades

Orders above the size threshold, drawn as bubbles on the chart. Big trades at an extreme with no follow-through: that side is trapped.

Speed of tape

Trades per second. A burst when breaking a range is ignition; a burst at the end of a long leg is climax.

Absorption on the tape

Large orders keep hitting the same price but price does not follow through.

Initiative on the tape

A string of large one-sided orders, price jumps through several levels in a row.

How to read

  1. In the last 5–10 ticks before the level: slowing speed supports a fade, accelerating speed supports a break.
  2. On touching the level ask one question only: do large orders hitting it make price follow through?
  3. Combine with the DOM: large orders fill but displayed size does not shrink, meaning there is a refill.
  4. After absorption, wait for a string of large orders from the opposite side: that is the sign of a side change.

Examples

A. Absorption at VAL

Three sell orders of 48, 62, 55 lots in a row at 25,411.00 (yellow) without price dropping. Then buy orders of 31 and 24 lots lift price (green). The sellers were absorbed, supporting a long fade.

B. Initiative breaking VAH

A string of 40–70 lot buys in under 1 second, price jumps 4 ticks through 25,430.00. On the retest only 3–6 lot sells appear. Supports a long break.

Do and don’t

Do
  • Always turn on the size filter, otherwise the NQ tape runs too fast to read.
  • Compare speed with the baseline for the same time of day.
  • Read the tape together with the DOM at the level.
Don’t
  • Treating a single large order as a signal.
  • Reading the tape mid-range when there is no level.
  • Trusting a speed spike during news as a signal.

Footprint: cluster chart

ATAS: Cluster chart, Bid × Ask, Imbalance. Used in phase 4, read only on closed M1 candles.

Footprint splits each candle into price levels and shows, at each level, how much volume was filled by aggressive sellers (bid column, left) and aggressive buyers (ask column, right). It shows how the candle was built.

Candle value area

70% of the candle’s volume around the POC. ATAS can shade this zone in the cluster. On the sample candle: 25,411.25 – 25,412.00. A close outside the candle’s value area shows the pushing side was strong until the end of the candle.

Max delta / Min delta

The highest and lowest delta reached while the candle was running (Cluster Statistic). Not visible on a closed candle, but shows which side’s force was reversed inside the candle.

Trades and time

Number of trades and the candle time. Large volume with few trades means large orders; large volume with many trades is a crowd of small orders.

Delta and Delta%

Delta = Σ ask − Σ bid, in contracts: net aggressive buying in the candle. Delta% = Delta ÷ Volume × 100, ranging from −100% to +100%: how dominant one side is. ATAS calls Delta% Delta/Volume in Cluster Statistic.

Step-by-step calculation with the sample candle

  1. Compute each level’s delta: ask − bid. This is the number on the ATAS horizontal bar.
  2. Add up the level deltas, or take Σ ask − Σ bid: 411 − 174 = +237.
  3. Candle volume = Σ bid + Σ ask = 585.
  4. Delta% = 237 ÷ 585 = 40.5%. Aggressive buyers take about 70% of volume (411/585), sellers about 30%.

Tip: Delta% = (ask share − bid share). 70% − 30% = 40%.

Delta% reading scale

≤ −30%One-sided selling
−30 … −15%Clear selling
−15 … −5%Leaning sell
−5 … +5%Balanced
+5 … +15%Leaning buy
+15 … +30%Clear buying
≥ +30%One-sided buying

Thresholds are system conventions, adjust them after backtesting. The ±15% threshold is the one used for the delta flip trigger.

Why you need Delta%, not just Delta

Candles A and B both have delta +210 but mean very different things. Candle A is a high-volume opening candle, +210 is only 5%: both sides almost balanced. Candle B is a low-volume lunchtime candle, +210 is 34%: buyers dominate. Absolute delta is for comparing with itself at the same time of day; Delta% is for comparing candles with different volume.

Read correctly
  • Always read Delta% with the candle direction: delta% against the body direction at a level is a sign of absorption.
  • Large Delta% on the candle breaking a level, in the break direction: initiative.
  • Per-level Delta% (e.g. POC: 77 / 159 = 48%) shows which level one side took over.
Easy to misread
  • Very large Delta% at the end of a long leg can be a climax, not continuation force.
  • Delta% is only the end-of-candle result; a candle closing at +5% may have reached +40% and been reversed (see max delta).
  • Comparing absolute delta between 9:30 and 12:00 is a wrong comparison.

Diagonal imbalance

Imbalance does not compare bid and ask on the same row. An aggressive buyer at price P fills against sellers at ask P, while an aggressive seller fills at bid P − 1 tick. Therefore:

  • Buy imbalance at P: ask(P) ≥ 3 × bid(P − 1 tick).
  • Sell imbalance at P: bid(P) ≥ 3 × ask(P + 1 tick).
  • Add a minimum volume threshold (e.g. 20 lots) to drop meaningless 9 × 1 cells.
  • On NQ you can merge 2–4 ticks per row for easier reading.

Basic patterns

Stacked imbalance

3 or more consecutive imbalance levels on the same side. An initiative zone, often becomes new support/resistance on the retest.

Absorption

1–2 extreme levels with unusually large volume from the attacking side, price does not follow through and the candle closes the other way.

Exhaustion

Attacking-side volume shrinks towards the extreme, the extreme has 0 on the attacking side. Nobody is chasing price any more.

Unfinished auction

The candle extreme still has volume on both bid and ask. Price often comes back to repair it. Use it as a target.

Finished auction

The extreme has 0 on the attacking side (e.g. a high with ask = 0). The auction on that side is over.

Trapped traders

Imbalance or big trades pile up at the extreme, then price closes the other way. That side is trapped, their stop-outs will push price faster.

Imbalance failure

Price closes through the stacked zone in the opposite direction. The old stacked zone flips into resistance/support.

P-shape / b-shape

Volume concentrated in the upper part (P) or lower part (b), the rest thin. Usually short covering or long liquidation.

Candle POC

POC at the end of a tail: price rejected there. POC in the body: price accepted.

The 3 triggers allowed for entry

Absorption
1–2 extreme levels with attacking-side volume of at least 60 lots and at least 2.5 times the average of the other levels in the candle. Price does not follow through, the candle closes in the opposite half.
Delta flip
The previous candle has clearly negative delta (around 15% of candle volume or more), the next is clearly positive and closes above the previous candle’s body high. The reverse for shorts. Only counts when it happens at a level.
Stacked
3 or more consecutive levels with imbalance ≥ 300% on the same side, each at least 20 lots. Trade in the stacked direction on the retest, do not chase price.
Not allowed
A single imbalance cell, a big-delta candle not at a level, a candle still running and not closed.

Delta and CVD

ATAS: Delta, Cumulative Delta, Cluster Statistic. CVD is used in phase 2, candle delta is part of the trigger in phase 4.

Delta = total volume filled at the ask − total volume filled at the bid, i.e. aggressive buying minus aggressive selling. CVD (Cumulative Volume Delta) is delta accumulated from the start of the session.

The numbers in Cluster Statistic

Delta
Σ ask − Σ bid of the candle.
Delta/Vol
|Delta| / Volume. Around 15% or more is a clear delta; below 5% is balanced.
Max / Min Δ
Highest and lowest delta while the candle is running. Candle 5: max +410 but closes +35, buyers pushed and were reversed within the candle.
Volume
Far above average at an extreme: climax or absorption.

Delta versus candle direction

Positive delta
Negative delta
Bullish candle
Bullish agreementAggressive buying pushes price up. Supports continuation, confirms a long break.
Selling absorbedSellers hit harder but price still rises. At support: a long fade signal.
Bearish candle
Buying absorbedBuyers hit harder but price still falls. At resistance: a short fade signal.
Bearish agreementAggressive selling pushes price down. Confirms a short break.

The two yellow cells only matter when they happen at a key level. Mid-range, delta against the candle direction is usually noise.

CVD divergence

Price makes a higher high (HH) but CVD makes a lower high (LH): aggressive buyers are weakening, price is lifted mainly by sell limits being pulled.

  • Fade: you want to see divergence as price enters the level.
  • Break: you want CVD to make a new high/low with price. If there is divergence, do not trade the break.
  • In a strong trend divergence can last many legs; only consider it at a swing or a level.
  • Reset CVD consistently. Resetting at 9:30 ET matches the VWAP RTH anchor.

Delta flip

A delta flip is when candle delta clearly changes sign right at the level and the next candle closes beyond the previous body. It is the fast version of CVD divergence over one or two candles. See the example "Delta divergence on a candle" in the 13 scenarios.

Combining at a TPO key level

VWAP is the filter gate, footprint is the mandatory trigger, CVD, DOM and tape decide the setup grade.

Fade at the level Responsive
VWAP
The level lies outside VWAP, ideally matching the ±1σ/±2σ band. The distance to VWAP is TP1 and must be ≥ 1.5 times the stop.
CVD
No new high/low with price as it enters the level.
DOM
Resting orders at the level hold or are added to; the opposite side thins out.
Tape
Large orders hit the level but price does not follow through, with refills.
Footprint
Absorption, delta flip or stacked imbalance in the opposite direction on the confirmation candle.
Plan
Enter when the trigger candle closes or on the retest. Stop 2–4 ticks beyond the absorption zone. TP1 VWAP/POC, TP2 the next TPO level.
Invalidation
2 M1 candles close through the level with delta in the break direction: the level is accepted.
Break qua level Initiative
VWAP
Price is on the break side of VWAP, VWAP sloping that way. The level is not at or beyond the ±2σ band.
CVD
New high/low with price on the break, no divergence.
DOM
Resting orders at the level get eaten or pulled; orders behind follow price.
Tape
A string of large orders through the level, speed spikes; on the retest opposing orders are small and sparse.
Footprint
Stacked in the break direction through the level, a clean retest or a delta flip on the retest.
Plan
Limit inside the stacked zone on the retest. Stop behind the stacked zone. Targets: unfinished auction, ±1σ/±2σ band, next level.

A candle closes back inside value with opposite delta: failed breakout, switch to fade.
